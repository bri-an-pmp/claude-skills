# Common RAE/DPD usage-dispute categories

A starting checklist for Mode 2 audits. This tells you what to look for — always confirm the specific instance against dpd.rae.es or dle.rae.es before flagging it, since correct usage can depend on context and regional variety.

  ## Grammar

  - **Dequeísmo / Queísmo** — adding "de" before "que" where it doesn't belong ("pienso de que...") or dropping a required "de" ("me acuerdo que..." instead of "me acuerdo de que...").
  - **Laísmo / Loísmo / Leísmo** — misuse of "la/lo/le" as direct/indirect object pronouns. Standard usage varies by region and RAE tolerates leísmo for personal "le" in some contexts — check DPD before flagging.
  - **Preposition regime errors** — verbs that take a specific preposition used with the wrong one ("consistir de" instead of "consistir en"; "quedar en verse" vs "quedar de verse").
  - **Gerundio de posterioridad** — using the gerund to describe an action that happened after the main verb rather than simultaneously with it (a very common error in news writing and translated text).
- **Agreement across long noun phrases** — gender/number agreement breaking down when a subject and its modifiers are separated by a long clause.
- **"Cuyo" misuse** — using "cuyo" where "que" or "del cual" would be correct, or failing to make it agree with the possessed noun.

## Orthography

- **Post-2010 accentuation changes** — "solo" (no longer written "sólo" as an adverb in standard use), demonstrative pronouns ("este," "ese," "aquel") without accents.
- **Prefix hyphenation** — most prefixes (ex-, co-, re-, anti-, pre-, etc.) now join the base word without a hyphen or space per the 2010 orthography, with specific exceptions (e.g., before another hyphen, an acronym, or a multi-word base).
- **Loanword adaptation** — spelling of adapted borrowings ("wifi," "escáner," "estrés") vs. keeping the foreign spelling; RAE generally prefers the Hispanicized form once one exists.
- **Capitalization** — RAE is more restrictive than English: months, days of the week, seasons, nationalities, and languages are lowercase in standard Spanish.

## Lexical

- **Anglicisms and calques** — words or phrases translated too literally from English ("aplicar para un trabajo" instead of "solicitar un empleo"; "asumir" used to mean "assume/suppose" rather than its traditional Spanish sense).
- **Word validity** — terms that sound plausible but aren't in the DLE, or are marked as regionalisms, informal, or discouraged.
- **False cognates** — words that resemble an English word but carry a different meaning in Spanish ("actualmente" ≠ "actually"; "embarazada" ≠ "embarrassed").

## Register and consistency

- Not a RAE "rule" violation, but worth flagging in a QA pass: inconsistent formality (mixing tú/usted/vos within one document without reason) or inconsistent regional vocabulary choices for a single target audience. Note these separately from genuine RAE-norm violations, since they're editorial rather than prescriptive issues.
